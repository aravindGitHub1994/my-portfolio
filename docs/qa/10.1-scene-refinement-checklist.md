## 0a · Fixes from the first run (session 23)

The owner ran this gate and reported everything else fine. Three findings, all
fixed; **each one invalidates a box that had already been ticked, so re-check the
three items named below.** Two were measurable and were measured; the third was a
diagnosis.

1. **The POST was silent.** Not broken — *inaudible*. Driving the real cue graph
   through a boot against a recording stub gave: degauss 0.36 of output with its
   whole fundamental at 38–92 Hz (a laptop driver reproduces none of it), the BIOS
   beep 0.118 for 100 ms, and the drive chatter — the only continuous sound in the
   phase, nine bursts across 2.7 s — at **0.041**, i.e. 2.5× quieter than a key
   clack. The chime, which *was* audible, sits at 0.21. So the beep and the
   chatter are levelled to ~0.21 and ~0.116, and the degauss gained a 232→104 Hz
   body plus an 800 Hz noise band, which is the part small speakers can actually
   move air with. Bus trims are untouched — `texture` is sheddable and shared with
   the clacks, so the correction is per cue. → **re-check §1.2 and §1.4.**
2. **The keyboard was a machine gun.** The rig tapped eight fingers on a 0.455 s
   mean interval — 17.6 taps/s, about twice the fastest human keystroke rate — and
   never stopped for the whole ride, so the clacks never stopped either. Thinning
   *at the audio end* could only ever produce a thinner continuous stream, and
   heavy thinning makes a metronome (the gate voices taps at its own period). So
   the fix is in `character/typing.ts`: the interval is now ~0.97 s (~8 taps/s) and
   the keyboard has bursts (1.8–4.5 s) and pauses (0.9–2.6 s). Simulated over two
   minutes: **3.1 clacks/s with 39 % silence, against ~11/s of unbroken typing**.
   Because §6.2 drives clacks from tap events, fixing the motion fixed the sound.
   → **re-check §3.1 and §3.3** (the fingers now visibly stop between bursts).
3. **Scrolling back from sign-off could not leave the dock.** Reported as "it
   doesn't undock until I click inside the desktop and then scroll again". The
   click was a red herring — the *pause* around it was the fix. `SCROLL_QUIET_MS`
   required every undock to follow a 350 ms gap, which the forward visitor always
   has (they stopped to read) and the backward visitor never does (they are
   travelling, so their events keep arriving inside the window and every one reads
   as the docking burst's momentum). §0's headless proof tested "a paused wheel
   gesture", so it could not see this. `MOMENTUM_MAX_MS` (1500) now bounds the
   pause rule: past it, scroll intent is honoured directly, because momentum has
   decayed by then and anything still arriving is a person. → **re-check §6.3 and
   §6.5, and §2.2 in the backward direction.**

## 9 · Things deliberately left alone — say if you disagree

Not defects; decisions or accepted costs. Each is cheap to reverse and none was
reversed without asking.

- **The painter's Gallery suggestion is unreachable.** The CRT can never show a
  window body — undocking requires every window closed or minimized, and the
  painter skips minimized windows. You chose to keep the ~90 lines (2026-07-30).
  ADR-013 §9a records it so no future slice is planned around painting a window.
- **The arm is nearly straight at the far end of every reach** (94–96 % extension
  for power, mouse and mug) because the shoulder is a fixed point. Levers if it
  ever reads as a mannequin: move the props closer, or let the shoulder
  *translate* — the second is a change to the rotations-only contract.
- **`lean` rests with the hands lifted and drawn back**, not on the desk: a pose
  move is one slerp with no arc control, so any hand travelling below keycap
  height sags through the keys.
- **Single-`scale` window drag has ~5 % x error**; keyboard undock can leave
  progress a few thousandths past the rest point.
- Self-noted nits nobody has flagged: shaft billboards pale edge-on, chair
  backrest plain boxes, faint CRT moiré.
- Committer identity resolves to your work email. Mentioned twice, left alone.
